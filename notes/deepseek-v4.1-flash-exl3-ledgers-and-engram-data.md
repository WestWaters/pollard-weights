# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (552B MoE, 40 × 384 experts) — EXL3 expert ledgers, hyper-connection gain curve, routing concentration and Engram table data from a 10× DGX Spark cluster

Contributed data, not a tool change. Measured while quantizing V4.1-Flash's routed experts to EXL3 (3–4 bit, exllamav3's `quantize_exl3`) for a 4-node vLLM serving recipe on DGX Spark (GB10), 2026-09-10. Pollard could not open this model (no HF/exllamav3 architecture class; no bf16 release — the checkpoint ships MXFP4 QAT experts + FP8 dense), so the capture ran DeepSeek's reference forward layer by layer and the quantizer was driven per expert from dumped activations. The method is Pollard's (measured Hessian sensitivity → budgeted allocation → MoE-aware export). The sanitized tooling is included under `experiments/dsv41_flash_route_b/` (experimental, as-is; see its README) so the numbers can be reproduced.

Files (`experiments/data/dsv41_flash/`):
- `dsv41_exl3_ledger_per_layer.csv` — per layer: hyper-connection write gain, EXL3 proxy error (tr(EᵀHE)/tr(WᵀHW), mean of 384 experts) for gate/up/down at K=3 and down at K=4, K4/K3 ratio, routing n_eff, top-1 expert share, dead experts (<64 routed tokens).
- `dsv41_hc_write_gain.json` — the gain per layer from two independent 39-row calibration sets (|hc_ffn_post|₂ / rms(stream)).
- `dsv41_routing_concentration.json` — routed tokens per expert for all 40 layers over 786,432 calibration tokens (top-6 routing).
- `dsv41_exl3_k4_over_k3.json` — down-proj proxy error at K=3 vs K=4 for 26 layers.
- `dsv41_presplice_nll_gate.json` — the reference-forward NLL with every routed expert replaced by its EXL3 reconstruction (all-K3).
- `dsv41_engram_access_and_fp4.json` — Engram n-gram table row-access frequency (in-sample and held-out coverage of top-N rows) and the NLL cost of fp4 rows.
- `capture_stats_rows_78_116/extra_L##.json` — per-layer capture statistics (activation absmax/median at the norm seams, hyper-connection mixing means, stream RMS, Sinkhorn/CSA2 mode, weight statistics) for one 39-row set.

## Findings a budgeted allocator can use
1. **Residual write gain must weight the ledger.** V4.1 mixes each block's FFN output into four residual streams through learned hyper-connection weights. The measured gain |hc_ffn_post| / rms(stream) is 5–7.5× at layers 0–13, ≈1 at 19–30 and 0.03–0.2 at 36–39; layer 15's FFN is nearly disconnected (0.39). Raw per-tensor EXL3 error *rises* with depth (w2 0.0028 → 0.0084), so tensor error alone would send extra bits to the layers whose output barely reaches the residual. Weighting by gain² concentrated the 3.5 bpw budget into layers 0–27 (down-proj K=4 on L0–14, 16–18, 20–27; gate/up K=4 on L0–14, 16–18, 20–22, 24, 25).
2. **K=4/K=3 EXL3 error ratio is constant: 0.25–0.27 across 26 layers** (down-proj). A mix can be planned from a single K=3 cook and only the picked tensors cooked at K=4 (we saved about half the K=4 compute).
3. **Down-proj carries 2.5–3× the error of gate/up at the same K in every layer** — the first extra bit belongs to w2.
4. **Tensor error and output NLL disagree by an order of magnitude.** With every routed expert at K=3 (3.01 bpw, 16.7 % relative weight error per matrix) the reference forward's NLL on 39 calibration rows moved by −0.0057 ± 0.0031 (16/39 rows worse). The 3.5 bpw mix (19 layers K=4/K=4, 3 K=3/K=4, 18 K=3/K=3; 3.51 bpw): ΔNLL −0.0032 ± 0.0055, 19/39 rows worse — also flat. Expert-output errors average out through top-6 routing and the residual. Caveat: these are the Hessian calibration rows; a held-out probe on the served model is the honest check (pending).
5. **Hessian convention for MoE down-proj barely matters**: route-weighted (g·a) vs unweighted (a) inputs differ by −0.4 % / +1.0 % on the two output-error metrics over a full layer (384 experts, same rows). Note for tooling: a quantizer's own `rfn_out` is computed on whichever rows it calibrated with, so passes with different weightings cannot be compared from their logs.
6. **Routing is far less concentrated than GLM-5.3's**: n_eff 56–197 of 384 experts per layer (GLM-5.3 L3: 18 of 256, 174 dead). Dead experts (<64 tokens of 786k) are 0–4 in early layers and grow to 26 in the last layer; they take the identity-Hessian/RTN fallback, never zeros.
7. **EXL3 on a 4.25-bit QAT grid behaves like a Gaussian control** (rfn_w 0.160 @3b, 0.081 @4b, ledgers within noise of a Gaussian-weights control). Re-quantizing an MXFP4 release to a 3–4-bit trellis is neither helped nor hurt by the grid.

## Lookup tables as a ledger object (Engram, 2 × 101 GB fp8, layers 1 and 14)
- fp4 rows cost nothing: fake-quantizing the gathered rows to MXFP4 (per-32 ue8m0) or NVFP4 (per-16 e4m3) inside the reference forward gives ΔNLL −0.0031 ± 0.0032 and −0.0025 ± 0.0031 while 80 % / 100 % of elements change (rel-RMS 0.109 / 0.094).
- Access frequency is Zipfian: over 786k tokens × 24 hash columns, 7.0 M of 384 M rows are touched per layer, 70 % of them once; top 1 M rows = 62 % of accesses, top 5 M = 89 % (in-sample). Held-out split (hot set from half the rows, tested on the other half): 41 % @1 M, 49 % @5 M, saturating at what the training half saw — corpus-bound, so a corpus-wide hash-frequency ledger (no forward needed) sizes the resident set. Both Engram layers address identical hash ids. Corpus-scale ledger (1.12 B tokens hashed, 26.8 B accesses and 106 M distinct rows per layer): held-out coverage 43 % @1 M, 59 % @5 M, 67 % @10 M, 74 % @20 M, 84 % @50 M, **92.7 % @100 M rows** (24.6 GiB fp8 per table, ~6 GiB per TP4 rank); the two Engram layers do NOT share ids (the earlier identical statistics were a coincidence of the hash design).

## Hardware profile (DGX Spark / GB10, 128 GB unified, one GPU per node)
Reference forward for calibration ~95 s/layer at 192 rows × 2048 (74 GB peak; expert-input dumps 0.82 GB/layer/node). EXL3 cook per layer (384 experts, exllamav3 LDLQ, weights over HTTP, Hessians built from dumps): K=3 all three matrices 57 min; down-proj only at K=4 16 min; gate/up only at K=4 30 min. Serving reference for the target recipe (shipped format, TP4): 42 tok/s single-stream aggregate, 131 tok/s at six streams, KV 1.16 M tokens at 80 % memory utilization.
